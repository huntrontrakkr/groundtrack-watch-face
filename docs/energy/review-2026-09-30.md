# Energy review, 30 September 2026

Where the three faces' instructions go, measured in the emulator
(`tools/energy/`, per-function attribution by `profile.mjs`), and what
follows. Instructions are a CPU proxy, not current; the display, flash and
radio are not modelled. The idle firmware background is the emulator
spinning on emulated devices and is left out.

## What a day costs now

App instructions, from one minute window and one build window per face
(Sun on Enroute; the ISS on Plotboard and Fuller, both on Green CRT, which
draws the terminator).

| | a minute | building the hour | a day (1,440 minutes, 24 builds) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Enroute, the Sun | 2.9 M | 112 M | 6.9 G |
| Plotboard, the ISS | 11.6 M | 103 M | 19.2 G |
| Fuller, the ISS | 35 M | 102 M | 53 G |

Fuller's minute had been guessed at 5 M; it is 35 M.

## Where a minute goes

| Enroute (2.9 M) | Plotboard (5.8 M per window, one window empty) | Fuller (35 M) |
|---|---|---|
| 40% software doubles (`__muldf3`, `__aeabi_dadd`, compares): `block_state` still decides each block's night in doubles, 228 rows × 13 blocks × up to 3 rows | 20% `class_at` (walking a row's runs to find one pixel's class) | 30% `fuller_dot`: a double *division* per height, and the terminator's `crosses` takes five heights a pixel |
| 12% `class_at` | 22% `above` + `crosses`: the terminator, five heights a pixel, in doubles | 35% doubles and conversions |
| 7% `clear` + 4% `letter`: the flag's and the callouts' halos | 16% `render` (the base pass over every masked pixel) | 12% `class_at` |

The base pass redraws every pixel of every 16-pixel block the terminator
band touches. What actually changes between one minute and the next is
far less:

| | pixels that change | pixels redrawn | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sun, Enroute | 220 (max 496) | 2,200 | 10× |
| ISS, world band, CRT | 208 | 11,630 | 56× |
| Moon, Night red (drawn terminator) | 977 | 22,333 | 23× |
| ISS, Fuller | 314 | 34,440 | 110× |
| Sun's day, Fuller | 632 | 28,021 | 44× |

(`native/host/harness -a` frames compared minute to minute; `-u` for the
pixels the update pass draws.)

## Where a build goes

| Enroute (112 M) | Plotboard (103 M) | Fuller (102 M) |
|---|---|---|
| 25% `__divdf3`: the relief smoother divides every pixel by 2 or 3, six times | 30% `map_cursor_next`: decoding 575 map rows, 828,000 cells, to sample 26,000 pixels | 37% `__aeabi_dadd`, 18% `__divdf3`: the grid sampled and smoothed in doubles |
| 25% `__aeabi_dadd`: smoothing and bilinear sums | 26% `__aeabi_dadd`, 17% `__divdf3` | 4% `fuller_locate`: which tile, per pixel, by barycentric doubles |
| 22% `map_cursor_next`: 139 rows, 200,000 cells, for a chart 100 cells wide | 9% `smooth_push`, 6% float↔double conversions | |
| 7% `material`: the chessboard distance to land, searched per pixel | | |

The map pack is 236 KB, of which 230 KB is relief. The plates use relief
only through ten thresholds (the shelf at −200 m; contours at 500, 1,000,
2,000, 3,000, 4,000 and 5,000 m; tints at 300, 1,000, 2,000 and 3,500 m).
Re-encoding the pack with each cell's *level* instead of its height:

| pack | bytes |
|---|---:|
| now (0.25°, 8-bit height codes) | 236,336 |
| 0.25°, land only | 6,073 |
| 0.25°, land + relief quantised to the ten levels | 30,157 |
| 1°, land + 8-bit relief (a mip level for the world band) | 29,749 |
| 1°, land only | 1,399 |

Rendered from quantised relief, every chart differs from today's by 250 to
1,500 pixels: contour lines and tint edges moved by a pixel
(`test-results/relief-quantised.png`, made by the review's script).

## The plan

Ordered by saving per effort. None of the first group changes the art.

### 1. The minute: draw the pixels that change

- Decide night entirely in integers. Heights are already Q30 (`fast_h`);
  `block_state`, `above`/`crosses` and Fuller's `fuller_dot` still go through
  doubles. Fuller's heights are int32 in 2^29 units: compare them with the
  thresholds in the same units, never divide.
- In a block the terminator band touches, compute each pixel's height at
  the old and the new minute (a few integer instructions each) and draw
  only the pixels whose zone differs, plus their four neighbours on a plate
  that draws the terminator. That turns the 10–110× overdraw above into
  about 1×.
- Fuller: bound a block-row's height by the grid points of the cells it
  crosses (the place along a row is affine, so those cells form a short
  staircase), so most blocks are known day or night without a per-pixel
  pass, as on the cylindrical charts.
- `class_at`: keep a small cache of decoded rows instead of walking runs for
  every lettering, halo and late-layer pixel.

Expected: Enroute 2.9 → ~0.6 M, Plotboard 11.6 → ~0.8 M, Fuller 35 → ~1 M
a minute. Over a day that is the largest term for every face.

### 2. The build: integers and a tiled, quantised map

- Smooth in integers (Q8 metres, the ÷2 and ÷3 as fixed-point multiplies)
  and sample in integers: per column the map cell and fraction, per row
  likewise, once per build; bilinear as integer lerps. Removes the
  divisions, most adds and every float↔double conversion (~45 M of Enroute's
  build).
- Re-pack the map as independently decodable 32×32-cell tiles with an
  offset table, cells holding the plate level (ten symbols: a table lookup,
  no escape codes, no binary search), plus 1/2° and 1° mip levels for the
  world band and the day chart; tiles that are one level and one class
  decode to a constant. Decoding falls from 200,000–830,000 cells to
  10,000–50,000, and the pack from 236 KB to about 45 KB with its mips.
  Plotboard needs only the 1° level; Fuller's GPS zoom needs only the land
  layer (6 KB in place of the 130 KB `land.bin`).
- The chessboard distance to land once per row with running minima rather
  than an 11×11 search per pixel.

Expected: builds from 100–112 M to 15–20 M. This changes the art by the
one-pixel contour moves shown above, and the study must sample and smooth
in the same integers.

### 3. Plotboard: a band that is built once

The world band's scale is fitted to the hour's longitudes (0.58–0.93 px a
degree across a day for the ISS and NOAA-20), so every hour rebuilds the
ground, and the sliding world (tape + world) rebuilds it every minute,
103 M a minute. With a fixed scale (the whole world across the band,
0.556 px a degree, or a chosen fixed zoom), the 360° band is built once
and an hour or a sliding minute is a column offset: the hour's build
draws only the route, stations and figures, and the sliding world costs
a minute's redraw. This is an art decision.

### 4. One renderer

The study (`enroute-render.js`, `chart-render.js`, `roll.js`, `fuller.js`,
`unfold.js`, `fuller-ground.js`, `fmath.js`) and the watch (`chart.c`,
`fuller.c`, `enroute_core.c`, `fmath.c`) draw the same picture twice, and
`native-scene.js` recovers the watch's class plane from a probe rendering.
About 1,500 lines mirror each other and the parity tests exist to keep
them so. Compiling the C core to WebAssembly for the study would leave one
renderer: the study keeps its page and controls and draws through it, the
parity tests and the scene format go, and every change above is written
once. The cost is that art is then iterated in C (a wasm build is
sub-second), and this machine needs `clang` and `wasm-ld`.

## Tooling

`tools/energy/measure.mjs` now takes `MEASURE_FACE` (enroute, plotboard,
fuller), sends the 25-byte settings, and points at the face's own
package; `tools/emulator-body.sh` takes `PEBBLE` for the pebble command
(a copy of `pb` that routes QEMU through `tools/energy/qemu-trace.sh`
traces this machine's emulator), and `tools/emulator-check.mjs` registers
every element set in `TLE_FILE`.
