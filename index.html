Read the chart

The large figure is the hour and the raised figure its minutes, set like a chart’s maximum elevation figure. The compass rose marks this hour’s station; the open triangle is the next hour’s reporting point. The magenta line is the route, bold behind the marker and fine ahead, with the quarter hours marked. Contours fall every 1,000 m, dotted at 500 m; offshore, the dotted blue line is the 200 m edge of the continental shelf. Three-letter codes are the tracking stations of NASA’s Mercury and Apollo networks.

THE CHART

Real ground, lightly drawn.

Relief comes from NOAA’s ETOPO1 and the USGS GMTED2010 elevation models, generalized and contoured like a jet navigation chart. The continental shelf edge is the real 200 m depth line. Small crosses mark every five degrees, and degree ticks run around the edge like a neatline.

THE ROUTE

Two fixes and a magenta line.

This hour is a VOR in its compass rose; the next is a reporting point. The route is the magenta line pilots follow on every modern display. The time borrows the chart’s maximum elevation figure: the hour large, its minutes raised.

THE NETWORK

Where the astronauts were heard.

Codes like TAN, CRO and GDS mark stations of the Mercury and Apollo tracking networks. For the ISS, each carries its acquisition circle, the reach of a low orbit, as drawn on the world maps of the first mission control rooms.

About this working study

This is a browser design study, not an installable watchface. Every watch pixel is one of the display’s 64 RGB222 colors. The map is north-up and equidistant cylindrical; for the Sun and Moon the hour’s two stations sit 128 pixels apart, and the ISS, which travels about 240° of longitude in an hour, is shown on a world band. Scenes are frozen until you interact: there is no animation loop, polling, geolocation or sensor use.

Coastlines are Natural Earth 1:50m land. Relief is a quarter-degree grid built from the AWS Open Data Terrain Tiles at zoom 3, whose sources there are NOAA ETOPO1 and USGS GMTED2010, both public domain; heights are smoothed before contouring and are not for navigation. Day, civil twilight and night come from the calculated Sun at the selected minute. Tracking-station positions are approximate, from public histories of the networks. Acquisition circles show where a 410 km orbit rises five degrees above each station’s horizon, about 15.6° of arc.

Sun and Moon positions come from Astronomy Engine. The ISS uses a bounded June 5, 2019 archive from Satellite.js and is not a live prediction. Figures are Fira Sans Medium and chart lettering is Departure Mono by Helena Zhang, both SIL OFL 1.1. Sources and credits.
