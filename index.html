THE COMPOSITION

A path that rises.

The lower numeral begins the hour; the upper one ends it. The orbit quietly leads the eye between them. Geography gives the face its character, while the marks do the work of telling time.

THE SURFACE

Type as a place.

Rounded hour towers emerge from the landscape, with sunlit roofs and deep sides. Their cast shadows follow the actual Sun. A single warm accent marks the present; an inlaid treatment offers a quieter alternative.

About this study

This is an art and legibility prototype for Pebble Time 2. Sun/Moon ground positions and coastlines are calculated from the same sources as the first study. The landscape is now viewed through a track-aligned perspective camera. Only two hour numerals are shown, with five-minute divisions between them.

Numerals are imagined, deliberately exaggerated towers above a spherical Earth. Their full volumes cast shadows along the actual solar direction onto the ground. Rounded edge normals soften their roofs; display grading and ambient contact shading keep the forms legible. A uniform ambient floor keeps the night side readable, and does not cast shadows. The compass follows geographic north in the camera view; it uses no heading sensor. Coastlines are flat map data; real elevation and terrain shadows are not yet modeled. Dither is optional, fixed in screen pixels and never applied to the time marks.

The camera, geography and numeral geometry are held for an hour. Lighting and shadows follow the selected minute. These caches demonstrate an update strategy, not measured watch battery savings. This is still a browser study, not an installable watchface.
