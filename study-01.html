THE CAMERA

A place to read.

The landscape holds still for a while. The marker advances along its time scale each minute. Then the camera moves to the next section of the journey. The Sun and Moon get a closer view; the ISS needs far more Earth.

THE PIXELS

Quiet texture. Clear marks.

One native pixel per dot. The shading pattern never changes randomly, and lettering stays outside the dither pass. Try the two-ink palette to study the form without color.

THE NEXT EXPERIMENT

Let the watch sleep.

Prepare orbital samples on the phone, store a compact batch on the watch, and move the marker from that cache. Measure map redraws and radio traffic before adding relief, shadows, or more tracks.

Study data, limitations & sources

This is a browser concept, not an installable watchface or a battery measurement. Natural Earth supplies the coastlines; Astronomy Engine calculates the Sun and Moon. The ISS uses a historical Satellite.js example at its June 2019 epoch because the current CelesTrak feed was unavailable during this study. It is always marked ARCHIVE and cannot be advanced to today.

Home is an editable-in-code Norfolk example, not your detected location. Cities are a small illustrative selection. The Moon icon uses its calculated illuminated fraction with a north-up waxing/waning convention; it does not yet rotate to your local sky. Horizon occlusion is real: hidden tracks are not drawn through the Earth. Minute marks are thinned when they would merge. No elevation data or terrain shadows are included yet.

All display colors lie on Pebble's RGB222 palette; the Two inks theme is strictly black and white. The browser keeps larger caches than the proposed watch implementation, so its memory and timing do not represent watch performance.
