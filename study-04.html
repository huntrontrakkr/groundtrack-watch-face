MATERIAL & LIGHT

Three landscapes

Warm limestone and teal water. Fired earth and periwinkle. Pale jade and deep teal. The same geography, with different color relationships.

Color between the colors

Neighboring pixels blend at reading distance, creating shades between the display’s 64 native colors. Switch it off to see the steps in the palette.

Read the landscape

The lower monument is this hour. The upper one is the next. Follow the fine line between them; each short division is five minutes. The small diamond carries the current minute.

AT READING SIZE

THE SURFACE

Color has depth.

Cool water meets warm stone. Broad color fields meet at softly mixed shorelines and colored shadows. Fixed pixel patterns carry the intermediate tones; the scene never shimmers to manufacture a color.

THE LIGHT

One Sun.

The calculated Sun lights the landscape, the raised numerals. The track and diamond remain flat graphic marks. A shadow keeps some of its material’s color. Ambient shading makes the monuments readable after sunset.

THE MAP

Earth, unfolded.

Fuller’s triangular projection provides the ground. A quiet crease marks an incompatible join; paired open diamonds mark a break in the orbit. The path never crosses a fictitious geographical bridge.

About this working study

This is a browser art study, not an installable watchface. All displayed watch pixels are opaque RGB222. Continuous color is rendered before fixed, spatial, linear-light color mixing. Geometry is cached within each hour. Solar shading changes only with the selected minute; there is no idle animation, temporal dithering, location polling or motion subscription. Native memory and electrical energy have not yet been measured.

The coastline is Natural Earth. Nearshore gradients suggest a material surface; they are not measured bathymetry. Towers are exaggerated imagined monuments, with sunlight intersections calculated in world coordinates. Small shadow penumbras and ambient fill are artistic display treatments. There are no measured terrain elevations or terrain shadows.

Sun and Moon positions come from Astronomy Engine. Moon observations are dated September 15, 18 and 20, 2026. The ISS uses a bounded June 5, 2019 archive from Satellite.js and is not a live prediction. The Gray–Fuller transform uses Philippe Rivière’s public-domain work. This repeated atlas is not the canonical Dymaxion net. Sources and credits.
