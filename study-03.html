THE GEOGRAPHY

A repeated Earth.

Fuller’s twenty spherical triangles unfold into an extended atlas. True neighbors join where they can; places repeat elsewhere. The marked cuts are part of the map, not hidden errors. A small familiar world helps locate you within it.

THE LIGHT

One material language.

Rounded hour towers keep their place in the landscape. Their surfaces and shadows use the same pair of inks as the Earth. The real solar direction lights each location, even when that triangle has been rotated or repeated.

THE CLOCK

Time is a trail.

Large numerals mark hours; short crossbars divide five minutes. The moving point carries the current minute. When the geography cuts, the path stops and resumes beside the matching letter. There is no line across the gap.

About the projection, light and battery

This uses the nonlinear Gray–Fuller triangle transform, with a numerical inverse, in Fuller's icosahedral orientation. It is a new repeated atlas layout, not the canonical Dymaxion net. Five faces meet at a vertex of an icosahedron; six equilateral triangles meet on a plane. Consequently a seamless global tiling would be misleading. Solid edges mark incompatible joins, and repeated places are intentional.

The Sun and Moon use Astronomy Engine. ISS is an explicitly archived June 5, 2019 demonstration using Satellite.js; it is not a current ISS prediction. City locations come from Natural Earth. Lights indicate the night side of this city catalog, not measured urban illumination. Home is a selected example, not your detected location.

Towers are oversized imagined monuments. Their volumes and shadows are evaluated on spherical ground; the resulting ground positions are mapped into Fuller triangles. Their display uses a local raised relief treatment. Coastlines carry no elevation data: real terrain shadows are not simulated. Solid light and dark fields meet at a narrow stippled terminator. Shadow boundaries have a small display penumbra. Roof lettering uses a contrasting ink, and recessed counters stay clear for legibility. These are deliberate display treatments, not additional light sources.

All watch pixels are opaque and use exactly two RGB222 colors. Dithering stays fixed to screen pixels. Geography, paths and tower geometry are cached; the page has no animation timer, location polling or motion subscriptions. This is a browser art study, not an installable watchface or a measurement of battery savings.
