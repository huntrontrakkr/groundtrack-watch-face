# Groundtrack 0.1.2

The settings page, rebuilt around what Groundtrack is: two faces in one app.

- **Face first.** Enroute is the hour as a chart; the Plotboard is the whole world with a time panel. The page asks which, then what it follows, then that face's own settings, then what both share. Each face keeps the body it last followed.
- **A body is on the face its orbit suits**, and the page says why the others are not: the fast, low satellites cross more of the world in an hour than Enroute's chart can hold; the Sun, the Moon, GPS and QZSS would hardly move across the Plotboard. The rule goes by the minutes a body takes a lap.
- **A setting is offered only where it changes the face.** The page showed every setting for every body, and many did nothing. A test now draws every value of every setting with the watch's own code to hold the page to it. A setting out of sight keeps its value.
- **Sections that fold**, each closed one saying what it holds. Plates and figures are tiles, not long lists.
- **Home by name.** Search for a town or city, use the phone's location, or enter coordinates. The search goes to Open-Meteo's place names service.
- **Events come from your calendar**, off by default. Give the page your calendar's private iCal link and the coming week's events stand on the route; the phone reads the link every three hours. Events are no longer typed in by hand, and with no link there are none. A watch face cannot read the phone's calendar itself. The link stays on the phone.
- **The preview** covers every satellite the phone has an orbit for, and QZSS's day and hour.
- On the Plotboard's ruler, the minute's mark is a flag or nothing (the time in full, chosen there, gave nothing).
- **GPS and QZSS no longer say EL OLD** on current orbit data. CelesTrak's newest sets for these high orbits are often two days old, which the watch took for stale.
