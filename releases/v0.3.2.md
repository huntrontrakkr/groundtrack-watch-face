# Groundtrack 0.3.2

Seventeen more plates, thirty-two in all, listed in an order that makes sense; and new ways of shading the land.

- **Night Side.** The Earth from orbit at night: by day a dim blue sea and teal land, at night both black with the coastlines lit gold.
- **Infrared.** The colour tables forecasters lay over weather-satellite infrared: violet lowland through orange to pale-yellow peaks, on a black sea.
- **Planetary.** The airbrushed Mars and Moon charts of the Apollo and Viking years, drawn over the Earth: ochre and salmon relief, a blue route.
- **Moonlit.** Airbrushed relief by moonlight: silver and slate on black. The dark companion to Airbrush and Planetary.
- **Vector.** No fills at all: the world in line work, one pale beam on black.
- **Graphite.** Spare grey instruments, the route the one white line.
- **Star Atlas.** Navy paper, cream coastlines, a pale gold route, after the engraved star atlases navigators used.
- **Rodger Young.** A fleet's tactical chart: khaki ground, a gunmetal sea, the line of advance in red.
- **Terrain.** An airliner's ground-proximity display: the land in dot densities of green, yellow and red on black, the lowlands left black, the route in the navigation display's magenta.
- **White Hot.** A thermal imager: the warm land light by day, the cold heights dark; by night the sea, holding its heat, brighter than the land.
- **Raster.** The night-attack head-up displays of the F-15E and F-16: infrared video as green raster lines, brighter with height, the symbology stroked over it.
- **Synthetic Vision.** A business jet's synthetic vision: terrain in earth tones on a grid mesh, the flight path magenta, the time panel the sky.
- **Bridge.** A ship's electronic chart, with its day, dusk and night colour tables: buff land and white deep water by day, near-black at night to keep the bridge's night vision.
- **Globus.** The painted navigation globe of the Vostok and Soyuz cockpits, with the panel its grey faceplate.
- **Engraved.** A copperplate engraving: the relief hatched by light, densest in shadow, the coasts waterlined.
- **Portolan.** The sea charts of the medieval and Renaissance navigators: one parchment for land and sea, coasts in brown ink and waterlined, the mountains stippled in gold leaf, the route in rubric red.
- **Vol de Nuit.** Saint-Exupéry's night airmail and the posters of its day: the world in midnight blue, the mountains picked out in gold like lights seen from the air, a vermilion route.
- **New shading:** the height bands of a plate can be drawn as density dots, line hatching, raster rows or a mesh, where before they were flat.
- **The plates are listed like with like:** paper and ships' charts, shaded and engraved relief, blue line drawings, tracking boards, mission control's glass and the cockpit's, single-ink displays, the Earth from space. A plate you had chosen stays chosen.
