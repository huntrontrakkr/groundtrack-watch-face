# Groundtrack 0.2.0

Any satellite, on the face its orbit suits.

- **68 satellites, in groups:** space stations, Starlink's versions (v1.0, v1.5, the polar shell, V2 Mini, Direct to Cell, this year's), other constellations, navigation (GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, BeiDou, QZSS, NavIC), weather (polar and geostationary), Earth observation, science and telescopes, and the old, odd and far (Vanguard 1, a Molniya orbit).
- **Any other satellite**, found from the settings page at CelesTrak by name, by catalog number or by a CelesTrak link. One chosen is kept under Your satellites.
- **The face follows the orbit.** Every body can be on the Plotboard. Enroute takes what its chart can hold; a satellite too fast for it is put on the Plotboard, and the page says why. An oval orbit's fast hour is drawn on the world band by the watch itself, so no hour breaks the face.
- **The Plotboard shows a slow body's whole day:** the Sun, the Moon, GPS and the rest that would hardly move in an hour cross the world marked in hours, with the hour in the panel as before. The route's ruler becomes the day's.
- **A ruler scaled to the route** on the Plotboard: each minute's mark stands over that minute's place on the orbit.
- **The Sun and the Moon marked beside** whatever is followed, on every face, if you ask for them.
- **The hour chart without its hour figures**, if you like: the route alone, the time in full beside the body.
- **Groundtrack Fuller: a scale bar**, if you ask for it, in the sheet's open space where the net leaves room.
- **The time is drawn over everything.** The minute flag and the time beside the body are no longer covered by home's mark or the margins.
- **The compass rose on the Plotboard too**, round the hour's start, as on Enroute.
- **The preview shows every choice, and stays in view.** The settings page makes it itself, for any body on any face, keeps the minute, and holds it at the top of the screen while the settings scroll. It is one picture now: as the watch's screen shows its colours, or at a tap as drawn.
- The Plotboard's band reaches further north and south where the world is drawn small, so a polar orbit stays on it; where it cannot, the route stops at the band's edge, clear of the date.
- Marks under the sliding tapes that bring the tape's minutes down to the route, as under the ruler.
- On the sliding world, the tape's numbers are no longer drawn twice and the date stays every minute.
- A geostationary satellite is a map of where it stands, not a chart zoomed past any map.
- A satellite with no orbit data to be had says NO ELEMENTS, whatever its orbit.
