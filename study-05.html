Extruded monuments, color mixing across whole fields and the rotated Fuller atlas. At 200 × 228 they became fringes, speckle and coastlines nobody recognizes. Here north stays up, so the land is familiar, and colors stay flat, so it stays quiet.

About this working study

This is a browser design study, not an installable watchface. Every watch pixel is one of the display’s 64 RGB222 colors. The map is a north-up equidistant cylindrical projection. For the Sun and Moon the hour’s two stations are placed 128 pixels apart; the ISS travels about 240° of longitude in an hour, so it is shown on a world band instead. Scenes are frozen until you interact: there is no animation loop, polling, geolocation or sensor use.

The coastline is Natural Earth’s 1:50m land, stored as a quarter-degree atlas and smoothed by bilinear sampling. The coast outline and the single offshore waterline are graphic devices, not measured depth. Day, civil twilight and night come from the calculated Sun at the selected minute; only the twilight band uses a fixed ordered screen. Numeral shadows are a short flat offset away from the Sun, a stylized height, not a terrain model.

Sun and Moon positions come from Astronomy Engine. The ISS uses a bounded June 5, 2019 archive from Satellite.js and is not a live prediction. Numerals are Fira Sans Medium (SIL OFL 1.1); minutes use the Dymaxion pixel figures. Sources and credits.
